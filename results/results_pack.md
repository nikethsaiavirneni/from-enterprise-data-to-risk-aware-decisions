Generated by `src/report.py`. Every number comes from files in `results/`; nothing is typed in. Cost ratios and the recommendation rules are experimental constructs, not retailer data or policy.

## 1. Data and split

- Source: M5 Forecasting Accuracy (community mirror github.com/Nixtla/m5-forecasts); files `sales_train_evaluation.csv`, `calendar.csv`, `sell_prices.csv` (SHA-256 of each file is in `run_log.txt`).
- Full data: 30,490 item-store series, 59,181,090 series-days (29 January 2011 to 22 May 2016). Sample: 1,000 items drawn at random, stratified by department (seed 42), all 10 stores, giving 10,000 series and 15,411,012 series-days after the rule below.
- On-shelf rule: keep series-day only if a price is listed in that week. In the full data it removes 20.8% of series-days, of which 0 had sales. Zero-sales share: 68.0% of all entries, 59.6% of kept entries (hence no MAPE/sMAPE).
- Memory cap 5.0 GB: estimated feature table 2.05 GB for the sample versus 6.24 GB for all series.
- Chronological split (never shuffled):

  - train: days 1-1759 (2011-01-29 to 2015-11-22), 13,335,174 rows at h=28
  - val: days 1760-1850 (2015-11-23 to 2016-02-21), 909,281 rows at h=28
  - test: days 1851-1941 (2016-02-22 to 2016-05-22), 910,000 rows at h=28

- Validation window contains the year-end holiday season; the test window does not.

## 2. Method specification

Forecast layer. Direct forecast for lead time h in {1, 28} days: ŷ(i,t) = f_h(y(i,s), s <= t-h ; z(i,t)), where z holds known-in-advance covariates of day t (listed price, calendar, events, SNAP, store and department codes). Every sales-derived feature uses only days <= t-h (unit-tested by corrupting later sales).

- Features per horizon: 23 (lag_28, lag_35, lag_42, lag_56, roll_mean_7, roll_mean_28, roll_std_28, roll_zero_28, price, price_ratio_7, price_rel_hist, listed_days, wday, month, day_of_month, week_of_year, event_type, is_event, snap, dept, cat, store, state).
- Lags: {h=1: [1, 7, 14, 28], h=28: [28, 35, 42, 56]}; rolling mean/std/zero-share over the last 28 observable days; warm-up of 60 days dropped.
- Baselines: seasonal naive (h=1: lag_7, h=28: lag_28) and the 28-day rolling mean.

Explanation layer. SHAP (TreeExplainer) on the XGBoost log-rate scale, on 20,000 random test rows per horizon. It describes what the model used, not causation.

Risk layer. On validation, forecasts are grouped into 10 deciles; each bin stores its residuals e = y - ŷ. A bin's relative uncertainty is u_k = sum|e| / sum(y) (its validation WAPE). Cases are Low / Moderate / High risk at the 33rd and 67th percentiles of u over validation cases. Bins, residuals and cut-offs are frozen and applied unchanged to test. (A first version used absolute residual spread and labelled near-zero forecasts Low; it was replaced after inspecting strata.)

Decision layer. Loss for order Q and demand y: l(Q,y) = Cu*max(y-Q,0) + Co*max(Q-y,0) with Co = 1 and Cu:Co in [1, 2, 4, 8, 16]; tau = Cu/(Cu+Co) is the newsvendor critical ratio. Policies on identical test cases: point Q = ŷ; global Q = max(0, ŷ + q_tau(all validation residuals)); risk-aware Q = max(0, ŷ + q_tau(validation residuals of the case's forecast bin)). Reported cost = sum l / sum y. Intervals: 95% percentile bootstrap, 500 resamples of series (sampling over series only).

Rule base (experimental). Demand level = High if ŷ >= validation 67th percentile of forecasts, else Low:

| Demand level | Risk | Priority | Action |
|---|---|---|---|
| High | Low | P1 | Stock to forecast; confident replenishment |
| High | Moderate | P1 | Stock to risk-adjusted quantity |
| High | High | P2 | Stock to risk-adjusted quantity; flag for human review |
| Low | Low | P4 | Minimal stock; routine replenishment |
| Low | Moderate | P3 | Stock to risk-adjusted quantity |
| Low | High | P3 | Small buffer; monitor demand signal |

Algorithm. (1) Fit f_h on train; predict validation and test. (2) On validation, bin forecasts and store residuals per bin. (3) Compute u_k and risk cut-offs; freeze. (4) tau = Cu/(Cu+Co). (5) For each test case find its bin, assign its risk label, set Q = max(0, ŷ + tau-quantile of the bin's residuals), emit priority and action from the rule base. (6) Score l once on test.

## 10. Claims the evidence does and does not support

Supported: forecast accuracy alone did not separate the approaches (a 28-day rolling mean is close); the validation-calibrated decision layer lowered assumed cost versus point and global policies; the gain held across both forecasters, bin counts and test sub-windows; calibration used validation data only.
Not supported / not measured: real retailer savings (cost ratios are assumed); multi-period inventory dynamics, lead times, capacity or service levels; results across several years, samples or seeds; any digital-transformation, innovation or entrepreneurship outcome (M5 holds no such data); causation from SHAP; the files come from a community copy of M5, not the original Kaggle download (their SHA-256 hashes are in `run_log.txt`; equality with the Kaggle files was not checked).

## 11. Reproducibility

Seed 42 throughout; `run_log.txt` holds environment versions, GPU, file hashes and per-stage results; `console_log.txt` holds the full console output including per-epoch LSTM training; 20 unit tests cover splits, leakage, metrics, risk labels and decision rules.

## 12. Implementation notes a paper should disclose

- The validation split is used three times: early stopping, XGBoost configuration choice and calibration (residual quantiles, bins, risk cut-offs). The test split is used once for scoring. Validation residuals can therefore be slightly optimistic; the coverage check in section 7 compares achieved coverage with the target.
- Days without a listed price are not kept as rows, but they enter lag, rolling and LSTM-sequence inputs as zero sales (the data show no sales on those days).
- RMSSE scale is the training one-step naive error computed on training rows after the 60-day warm-up.
- Price and calendar covariates of the target day are assumed known at order time (planned prices).
- The Wilcoxon test is on per-case squared errors with a normal approximation; cases are not independent (repeated series and days), so the p-value is descriptive.
- `recommendations_sample.csv` is a seeded sample of the rule-base output; full counts per priority and risk stratum are in `run_log.txt`.
