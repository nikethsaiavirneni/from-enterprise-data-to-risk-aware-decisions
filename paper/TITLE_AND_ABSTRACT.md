## Title

From enterprise data to risk-aware decisions: a knowledge-based AI framework for digital transformation of retail operations

## Abstract

Forecasts are inputs to operational decisions, yet retail analytics often stops at point predictions. This short communication develops a knowledge-based AI framework that turns enterprise sales data into auditable replenishment decisions through four linked layers: a demand forecast, SHAP attribution, validation-calibrated risk strata, and an explicit rule base with a quantile order policy. We evaluate it on 10,000 item-store series from the public M5 retail data, using a chronological split and lead times of 1 and 28 days. On the held-out test window, gradient-boosted trees and a small LSTM reached an RMSE of 1.82 and 1.81 (1 day) and 2.05 and 2.03 (28 days), against 2.51 and 2.64 for a seasonal-naive benchmark, while a 28-day rolling mean was close behind, so forecast accuracy alone did not separate the approaches. Decision quality did. Under an assumed 8:1 understock-to-overstock cost ratio, risk-aware ordering lowered cost per unit of demand by 36% relative to ordering the point forecast (95% bootstrap interval 35.5–36.5%) and by 14% relative to a single global correction, with all calibration fitted on validation data only. Cost ratios are assumptions, not retailer data, and the study does not measure digital-transformation outcomes. Code and tests are public.

- Calibration uses validation data only; the test window is scored once
